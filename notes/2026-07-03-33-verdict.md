# 2026-07-03 — #33 verdict: kernel scaling under repeated cubic folds

> **Status:** the first Verdict below is superseded by the later update blocks — final numbers and the final ceiling are in the LAST update. Kept as measurement history.

Measured after the qqbar backend (ADR 0013), `scratch/probe.ml` (and its
split variants `probe_stack.ml` / `probe_case3.ml` / `probe_round3.ml`, used
only to let the slow cases run past a single foreground timeout — same
`Geom.beloch7_creases` calls, same inputs, independent per case):

- axiom 7, rational coords: 0.000s (baseline, unchanged)
- axiom 7, p.x = √2/4: **~48s** (three runs: 48.825s, 48.028s, 47.455s) — was
  >120s timeout (never completed). A real, large improvement, but nowhere
  near the "sub-second" expectation the task brief anticipated.
- axiom 7, p.x = (√2+√3)/8: **did not complete within 900s** (7.5× the
  original 120s budget; killed via `timeout 900`, exit 124). Previously this
  case was interrupted/never measured at all — it is now measurably *worse*
  in wall-clock terms than the old 120s ceiling, just with a much higher
  ceiling to hit.
- stacked cubics:
  - round 1: 0.000s
  - round 2: 0.000s
  - round 3: **did not complete within 900s** (killed, exit 124)
  - rounds 4–5: not reached (blocked by round 3)

## Verdict

The 20-step budget from #33 is **not met**. Only 2 stacked cubics are
interactive; round 3 already exceeds a 900s budget with no sign of finishing
(memory climbing, CPU pegged the whole time — this looks like genuine
combinatorial blowup, not a hang).

ADR 0012's scaling concern is only **partially** addressed by ADR 0013, and
not where #33 actually lives. ADR 0013 replaced `Num`'s raw arithmetic
(`add`/`mul`/`div`/`sqrt`/`compare`) with FLINT `qqbar`, which is genuinely
fast (the spike note `notes/2026-07-03-flint-kernel-spike.md` measured
sub-millisecond ops even at degree 27). But `Num.real_roots` — the routine
`Geom.beloch7_creases` calls to solve axiom 7's cubic `F(t)` — only uses that
fast path when every coefficient is already rational (`lib/num.ml:349-357`).
As soon as one coefficient is irrational (any of our benchmark cases past the
rational baseline), `real_roots` falls back to ADR 0012's original
resultant-elimination pipeline over multivariate polynomials
(`lib/num.ml:358-520`, `Mpoly.resultant` per generator) — exactly the
pipeline whose ">120s, killed" cliff on cross-field comparisons is documented
in the spike note. ADR 0013 never rewired `real_roots` itself onto qqbar's
native root-finding; it only fixed the *arithmetic* underneath it. So the
degree-2 input (√2/4) still pays ~48s of resultant elimination, the degree-4
compositum ((√2+√3)/8) and the round-3 stacked case (whose input is itself an
algebraic number produced by round 2's crease, likely already
higher-degree) hit the same wall ADR 0012 always had — just with more
headroom (900s tested vs. 120s before) before declaring it unusable.

Remaining ceiling: **`Num.real_roots`'s Mpoly resultant-elimination path for
non-rational coefficients**, which shows up first at axiom-7 inputs beyond a
single quadratic generator and, in the stacked-cubic benchmark, at round 3.
Rewiring `real_roots` to solve directly in `qqbar` (which the spike measured
at ~1000× our speed on comparable cliff cases) is the natural next step, but
is out of scope for this note per the task brief — flagging it here per that
brief's "revisit only if the verdict note says so."

## Update (same day): H(Y)/certify removed — qqbar-verified candidates

The first measurement above blamed the whole non-rational path; a follow-up
fix removed the part of it that the √2/4 case was actually stuck in.
`real_roots`'s algebraic-coefficient branch no longer builds the H(Y)
separation-bound polynomial nor runs the interval `certify` loop: R's
candidate roots come exactly from FLINT (`Qqbar.real_roots_of_poly`) and are
kept iff P evaluates to exactly zero at them in qqbar. Degree-≤3 survivors
are upgraded back to the `Field` fast path (`field_upgrade`), preserving
cube-root.bel's representation. Full suite green (158 tests).

| axiom 7, p.x = √2/4 | ~48s | **0.006s** |
| axiom 7, p.x = (√2+√3)/8 | >900s, killed | **still >600s, killed** |
| stacked cubic round 1 | 0.000s | 0.000s |
| stacked cubic round 2 | 0.000s | 0.000s |

| stacked cubic round 5 | not reached | not reached |

### Where the remaining time goes (instrumented per stage)

The H(Y)/certify wall is gone — what remains is **building R itself**, i.e.
the part the fix deliberately kept. Instrumented breakdown:

- **√2/4** (1 generator, degree 2): eliminate R (deg 6) 0.000s, squarefree
  0.000s, FLINT roots 0.000s, qqbar filter 0.004s. The old 48s was entirely
  H(Y)+certify; removing it gives the expected sub-second result.
- **(√2+√3)/8** (√2+√3 has degree 4; the cubic's coefficients register as
  **3 distinct quartic generators** — the affine-merge optimisation only
  collapses degree-2 pairs): resultant steps v=1 0.000s, v=2 0.084s,
  **v=3 never finishes** (>119s CPU when killed). Sylvester of a deg-16-in-v₃
  intermediate against a quartic minpoly, expanded by Laplace determinants.
  The earlier ">900s" for this case was *also* stuck here, not in H(Y) —
  the first measurement's "R is cheap" claim only held for the
  single-quadratic-generator case.
- **stacked round 3** (3 generators of degree 3): eliminate R (deg 81)
  36.3s, **`Poly.squarefree_part` on R 191.4s** (naive ℚ-Euclid gcd with
  coefficient swell on the fat deg-81 resultant output), FLINT real_roots
  0.015s, qqbar filter 0.029s, field_upgrade 0.000s.
- **stacked round 4** (3 generators of degree **9**): step v=1 0.958s, killed
  in step v=2 — an 18×18 Laplace determinant over huge Mpolys. Hopeless by
  construction: R would have degree 3·9³ = 2187.

### #33 answered directly

- **Degree growth per round**: the fed-back coordinate's degree triples each
  round — round-3 inputs produce generators of degree 3, round-4 of degree 9
  (3^(k−2) at round k), and each round's cubic carries ~3 distinct such
  generators, so R's degree is 3·d³: 81 at round 3, 2187 at round 4.
- **Practical ceiling, current kernel**: axiom-7 inputs over a *single
  quadratic* extension are now effectively free (0.006s); inputs whose
  coefficients span several generators of degree ≥ 3 hit the R-construction
  wall — round 3 costs ~4 min (not interactive), round 4 is out of reach.
  The interactive stacked-cubic ceiling is **round 2**; the #33 20-step
  budget remains unmet.
- **Named bottlenecks, in order**: (1) the iterated `Mpoly.resultant`
  Laplace-determinant chain when ≥ 2 generators of degree ≥ 3 are present;
  (2) `Poly.squarefree_part`'s naive ℚ-gcd on the fat R (191s of round 3's
  229s). Cheap follow-ups, flagged not done: FLINT's
  `qqbar_roots_fmpz_poly` factors internally, so the OCaml-side squarefree
  step is removable (dedupe roots via `sort_uniq cmp_re`) — that alone would
  cut round 3 to ~37s; merging coefficients into a single generator
  (generalising the degree-2 affine merge / primitive element) would shrink
  the resultant chain; bypassing R entirely via Calcium root-finding on
  algebraic-coefficient polynomials would remove the wall outright.

## Update (2026-07-03): interpolated Mpoly resultant + exact root dedup

Both cheap follow-ups from the previous update are now implemented, and they
attack the two named bottlenecks directly:

- **(1) `Mpoly.resultant` no longer expands a symbolic Sylvester determinant
  by Laplace** (factorial in matrix size). Instead it evaluates the remaining
  variables on a rational grid, computes each specialized Sylvester
  determinant numerically over ℚ by Gaussian elimination (`Poly.det`), and
  Lagrange-interpolates the multivariate result. Same object exactly: the
  matrix layout is fixed by the generic v-degrees `(da, db)`, so specializing
  commutes with taking the determinant even where leading coefficients vanish
  at a sample point. Per-variable degree bound `db·deg_x(a) + da·deg_x(b)`.
  `Mpoly.det` deleted (`resultant` was its only caller). This is what killed
  the (√2+√3)/8 wall and collapsed round 3's elimination.
- **(2) The OCaml-side `Poly.squarefree_part r` before FLINT is gone.**
  `Num.real_roots` now hands the raw (non-squarefree) superset R straight to
  `Qqbar.real_roots_of_poly`, and that wrapper deduplicates its output with
  `List.sort_uniq cmp_re` (exact: `cmp_re = 0` iff the algebraic numbers are
  equal). FLINT factors internally, so the 191s ℚ-gcd pass is simply removed
  with no change in the returned root set. Full suite green (46 test groups
  incl. `mpoly`, axiom-7, real-roots, field, cube-root e2e — identical
  expectations, the resultant is the same polynomial).

|---|---|---|

The (√2+√3)/8 case — three distinct quartic generators, the case that never
finished under Laplace — now completes in **28s**: the third `resultant`

squarefree pass and the 36s Laplace elimination are gone).

### #33 answered directly, updated

- **Degree growth is unchanged and intrinsic**: the fed-back coordinate's
  degree still triples each round; R has degree `3·d³` (81 at round 3, 2187
  at round 4). The fix makes the *elimination* polynomial in the matrix size
  instead of factorial, but it cannot change how big R itself is.
- **Practical interactive ceiling now**: single-quadratic axiom-7 inputs are
  free (0.006s); multi-generator degree-4 inputs like (√2+√3)/8 are ~28s —
  usable but not interactive. For stacked cubics the interactive ceiling
  moved from **round 2 to round 3** (0.83s, comfortably interactive). Round 4
  (R of degree 2187) still walls past 600s — the resultant is now cheap per
  determinant, but the *number and size* of grid samples needed to
  interpolate a degree-2187 result is itself the wall. Genuinely combinatorial
  by construction; only representation-level changes (primitive-element
  merging of generators, or Calcium root-finding directly over
  algebraic-coefficient polynomials to bypass R entirely) can move it.
- The #33 20-step budget **remains unmet** (ceiling round 3), but the two
  measured software walls — Laplace resultant and the ℚ-gcd squarefree pass —
  are both eliminated; what is left is the intrinsic `3·d³` degree growth of R.

## Update (2026-07-03): FLINT 3.6 roots-first — R bypassed for degree ≤ 3

The "bypass R entirely via Calcium root-finding on algebraic-coefficient
polynomials" follow-up flagged above is now implemented. The flake overrides
`flint3` to **3.6.0** (nixpkgs is still on 3.5.0), which adds
`_qqbar_roots_poly_squarefree` — roots of a squarefree polynomial with qqbar
coefficients, with internal degree/bits limits (we pass 10000/100000; it
returns 0 instantly when the coefficient field is too big, our fallback
signal). `Num.real_roots`'s algebraic branch now tries FLINT first for
degree ≤ 3 polynomials whose discriminant is exactly nonzero in qqbar
(squarefreeness precondition, checked directly: b²−4ac / the cubic Δ);
returned candidates are still filtered by the exact `eval_p` zero check, so
no spurious root can leak. On FLINT's `None` (limits) or a zero
discriminant, the interpolated-resultant Mpoly elimination path runs
unchanged.

|---|---|---|
| axiom 7, rational coords | 0.000s | 0.000s |
| axiom 7, p.x = √2/4 | 0.006s | 0.005s |

The (√2+√3)/8 case — three quartic generators, 28s of interpolated
elimination — is now 0.113s: FLINT solves the cubic directly over the
degree-4 compositum, no R at all. Round 3 drops 0.83s → 0.068s the same way.
Round 4 (coefficients over degree-9 generators) still walls within a 300s
budget: whether FLINT's limits reject it (falling back to the hopeless
deg-2187 R) or its internal computation is itself too heavy, the outcome is
unchanged — killed at the timeout with no output. The #33 20-step budget
remains unmet (interactive ceiling still round 3, now ~12× faster there);
full suite green, incl. a new sub-second regression test for the
(√2+√3)/8-style cubic.

## Update (2026-07-03): primitive-element tier landed

`Num.real_roots` now has a Tier-3 primitive-element path (spec + spike:
`notes/2026-07-03-calcium-pe-spike.md`). Coefficients spanning a compositum of
independent folds are expressed over one generator γ (FLINT
`qqbar_express_in_field`) and eliminated with a single `R = Res_x(μ_γ, F̃)` of
degree `3·[ℚ(γ):ℚ]`.

**Measured, this branch:** a cubic with a single degree-8 coefficient
(`√(√2+√3)`), which qqbar-native root-finding took ~9s on in the spike, now
solves in **~0.5s** through the tier (`tests/test_num.ml`,
`test_real_roots_deep_stack_fast`); independent-fold and degree-≥4 cases route
through the tier and verify exactly.

**Estimated, not yet directly benchmarked through the landed path:** the #33
round-4 tripling-stack case (coefficients over a degree-9 field), previously
`>300s killed`, is projected at ~0.5–2s from the spike's degree-scaling curve
(`notes/2026-07-03-calcium-pe-spike.md`). That spike measured a *doubling*
stack via a standalone script, so the figure is an extrapolation to the
*tripling* round-4 case, not a direct measurement. On that basis the
interactive stacked-cubic ceiling is expected to move from round 3 to ~round 5;
the intrinsic `3·d³` degree growth is unchanged, so a wall persists beyond that.

The Mpoly multi-generator path remains as a correctness-preserving fallback.
Calcium (`ca_t`) was evaluated and rejected — it caps out below the status quo.
