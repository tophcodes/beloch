# 2026-07-03 — Two layers: capability vs. surface ergonomics

Came out of a research question: which Huzita-Justin axioms are actually used in
real (decorative) paper folding, vs. only in mathematical constructions?

## The frequency finding

Reference-based folding maps to axioms as *"define a crease from existing
points/edges."* Ordering by how often the fold shows up in real models /
Yoshizawa-Randlett diagrams (Huzita-Hatori / Wikipedia numbering):

- **O1, O2, O3, O4** — ubiquitous to common. Line through two landmarks; corner
  onto corner (⟂ bisector, the "book fold"); edge onto edge/crease (angle
  bisector); perpendicular through a point.
- **O5** — occasional. Swing a corner onto an edge, crease pinned through a
  point.
- **O6 (the cubic Beloch fold)** — **essentially never in decorative folding.**
  It is a *neusis* (slide the paper until two alignments hold simultaneously),
  in general three solutions, solves general cubics → trisection, cube doubling.
  Lives in math constructions only. Hull notes O6/O7 are painful to perform by
  hand without unfolding.
- **O7** — rare.

Sources: Wikipedia Huzita-Hatori; arXiv 2312.10780 (Beloch curve / real cubics);
Hull *Origametry* (2020) ch. 1-2 (`refs/hull2020.txt`, ll. ~988-1162, §2.3). The
fine ordering (O1 vs O2 vs O3) is reasoned from degree + diagram convention, not
a measured corpus count — flagged as such.

## The design consequence: two layers, always

Beloch has to serve two masters at once, and they must not be conflated:

1. **Capability layer — every axiom ①–⑦ is expressible**, bounded only by
   *algebraic computability* (the number kernel ℚ → √ → ∛; see spec §3/§6). This
   is the whole point of the language: the cubic Beloch fold ⑦ is a first-class
   citizen even though nobody folds it for fun. Completeness of the axiom set is
   non-negotiable.

2. **Surface / ergonomics layer — sugar for the folds people actually make.**
   The common axioms deserve short, intuitive syntax; the exotic ones can stay
   verbose. This is *purely* a surface-syntax concern and changes nothing about
   the semantics or the kernel.

The language stays *exact and complete* underneath; the sugar just makes the
90%-case pleasant.

## The trap: "1–4 & maybe 5" is numbering- AND axis-dependent

Two different axes are in play and **they do not coincide**:

- **Fold frequency** (what should get sugar): high for O1-O4, medium O5.
- **Algebraic degree** (what the kernel cares about): ①-④ degree 1 / stay in ℚ,
  ⑤⑥ degree 2 / √, ⑦ degree 3 / ∛.

Justin numbered the axioms *by algebraic power*, so Beloch's own numbering tracks
the **degree** axis, not the frequency axis. Cross-map (authoritative table in
spec §1):

| Beloch/Justin | fold | Huzita/Wiki | fold frequency | kernel tier |
|:---:|---|:---:|---|:---:|
| ① | line through two points | O1 | ubiquitous | ℚ |
| ② | point onto point (⟂ bisector) | O2 | ubiquitous | ℚ |
| ③ | perpendicular through a point | O4 | common | ℚ |
| ④ | project a point onto a line | O7 | **rare** | ℚ |
| ⑤ | line onto line (angle bisector) | O3 | **ubiquitous** | √ |
| ⑥ | point onto line, crease through a point | O5 | occasional | √ |
| ⑦ | cubic Beloch fold | O6 | never (math only) | ∛ |

So the request "sugar for axioms 1–4 & maybe 5" resolves *differently* by scheme:

- Read in **Beloch numbering** → sugar {①②③④} + maybe ⑤. That is the
  **degree-1 rational tier** + first √. But it sugars ④ (project-onto-line,
  *rarely folded*) while leaving the *ubiquitous* angle bisector ⑤ as only a
  "maybe." Ergonomically backwards.
- Read in **Wikipedia numbering** → sugar {O1 O2 O3 O4} + maybe O5. That is the
  **frequency-common** set — the bisector (O3) is in, the rare project fold (O7)
  is out. Ergonomically right.

**Provisional stance:** the sugar layer should follow **fold frequency**, not the
kernel tier — sugar exists to make common folds pleasant, and commonness is a
folding fact, not an algebraic one. In Beloch numbering that means the
sugar-worthy core is **{①, ②, ③, ⑤}** (+ ⑥ maybe), *not* {①②③④}. Note ④ drops
out and ⑤ moves in — exactly the opposite of a literal "1–4" reading.

**Open question for next session:** confirm which axis drives the sugar boundary.
If frequency (my recommendation), the sugar set is {①②③⑤ (+⑥)}; the "1–4"
phrasing was the degree-ladder talking. If there's a reason to bind sugar to the
rational tier instead (e.g. "sugar = folds we can render without √"), say so —
that is a defensible but different design, and it needs stating explicitly.

Either way: **capability layer = all seven, always.** Only the surface set is up
for debate.
