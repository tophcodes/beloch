# 2026-06-30 — Axiom 6 (Justin ⑥)

Axiom 6 ships: `map .p onto --d through .p'` (`toward .x` for the second
solution). Fold .p onto line .d with the crease pinned through the fixed point
.p'.

## Numbering — resolved

Beloch follows strict Justin numbering (verified against the §8.1 table in
`refs/justin1986.md`: axioms 1–5 = Justin ①–⑤). So **axiom 6 = Justin ⑥**
`(P → D, P' → P')`, the **second-degree** operation (0/1/2 solutions). The
project's earlier shorthand ("axiom 6 = the cubic") conflated ⑥ with ⑦ and was
wrong — the warning in `notes/2026-06-29-axiom5.md` was right. The cubic Beloch
fold is Justin ⑦ → Beloch **axiom 7**, a future slice needing a real-algebraic
number kernel (cube roots; casus irreducibilis defeats real radicals).

## Stays in the quadratic tower

The pivot .p' is on the crease, so it is equidistant from .p and its image — the
image lies on the circle (centre .p', radius |p'p|) ∩ .d. Circle∩line is a
quadratic: one `Num.sqrt` on the discriminant, no cube root. `Num` is untouched.
[justin1986 §8.2c, l.156]

## Construction

`Geom.circle_line_intersection centre r2 d` → 0/1/2 points; `Geom.beloch_creases
p d p'` maps each landing to `perpendicular_bisector p landing`, dropping any
landing equal to p (the identity, when p ∈ d). Eval resolves `toward .x` by
nearest landing (exact squared distance) and raises out-of-reach / ambiguity /
same-point errors.
